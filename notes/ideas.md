# Ideas

Raw ideas, newest at the bottom.

## 16 Nov 2026, first brainstorm (Joseph)
1. **Why fans are absent matters, not just whether.**
   - Imposed absence (Covid ghost games, closed-door punishments, capacity caps): the crowd is gone but the team's mood is not obviously affected. Clean test of the crowd itself.
   - Voluntary absence (fans not turning up, protests, boycotts, empty seats in a bad season): low turnout may both reflect and worsen morale. Confounded with form and team quality, so it needs controls.
   - Measure "how full" as well as "how many": occupancy = attendance / capacity.
2. **Stadium noise is the main interest.** Ideally link noise to outcomes. Needs a definition first:
   - absolute loudness (dB) vs loudness relative to that ground's normal
   - home noise vs away-end noise
   - noise at key moments (penalty shouts, decisions, late pressure) vs average
   - mechanisms noise could act through: referee decisions (fouls, cards, penalties, added time), home players, away players
3. **Brighton (Tony Bloom's club) as a case-study subset.** Amex Stadium, Premier League since 2017/18. Only 19 home games a season, so it illustrates rather than proves. Use the wider dataset for the statistics and Brighton for the story.

## Data status (16 Nov 2026)
- football-data.co.uk: results, Pinnacle and other odds, shots, fouls, cards, corners, referee (top divisions). Pulled: Premier League, Championship, Bundesliga, La Liga, Serie A, Ligue 1, 2015/16 to now.
- Transfermarkt matchday pages: attendance and referee per match. Scraping Premier League 2015/16 to now. Ghost games show no attendance.
- FBref and worldfootball.net: blocked from here.
- Noise: no systematic match-level decibel dataset exists publicly. Only one-off surveys (FanChants 2011 season averages, Press Association smartphone readings 2014) and a 2026 arXiv paper on how to measure crowd noise properly. Needs proxies or our own measurement.
